Autonomous wheelchair for MND patients

A voice-controlled power wheelchair that maps and navigates indoors on its own, built for people with ALS and other motor neuron diseases.

Problem

People with motor neuron diseases such as ALS gradually lose control of their hands and, over time, their speech. A power wheelchair that can navigate indoors on its own and take voice commands, even as speech changes, can extend their independence.

What I built

The hardware and software stack for an autonomous wheelchair, under Dr. Edward Grant:

  • A test bench on an electric wheelchair with 2D LiDAR, an RGB-D camera, wheel encoders and an IMU, running ROS on Raspberry Pi and Ubuntu for remote control and monitoring.
  • Autonomous control with SLAM on the 2D LiDAR, and vision-based obstacle avoidance with a MobileNet model.
  • Voice control: a TensorFlow network for speech accent classification, deployed on IBM Watson, plus sound tracking using direction-of-arrival estimation.

How it works

The wheelchair builds a map of the building as it drives and keeps track of where it is in that map. To choose the right SLAM approach, I compared five algorithms, Gmapping, Hector, RTAB-Map, VINS-Mono and RGBD-SLAM, on localization, mapping accuracy and usability for patients with differing conditions.

Results

RTAB-Map was the most scalable: it worked across different sensor combinations and estimated the robot’s location accurately and consistently across a range of speeds. The comparison became my M.S. thesis and a paper at the 2019 IEEE International Conference on Cyborg and Bionic Systems in Munich. The poster on the voice-controlled system won 3rd place at the NC State ECE Graduate Research Symposium in 2018.
